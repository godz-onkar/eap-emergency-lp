Layout and build notes

The page has one job: get a homeowner with a plumbing emergency to call. Everything below is built around the phone number. When you give feedback, name the section number.

How the page is built

  1. Header (sticky). Logo left, tap-to-call number right with "Open 24/7". Stays on screen while scrolling.
  2. Hero. Headline matches the emergency ad: "24/7 emergency plumber in Wilmington, NC". One yellow call button, a callback link for people who can't talk, then the rating, years in business and license number. On mobile the whole hero fits the first screen with no photo, so the call button is above the fold. On desktop a real job photo sits on the right.
  3. "Water still running?" A short do-this-now note: shut off the main valve, then call. Right under the hero, overlapping its bottom edge on desktop.
  4. What happens when you call. Three steps: you call any hour, a licensed plumber comes to you, you get the price before work starts. The navy line with elbows is the page's pipe motif. East Atlantic truck photo and a call button.
  5. Reviews. 4.8 stars from 600+ Google reviews and four real published reviews, each tagged by situation (emergency at home, after hours, leak repair, business emergency), plus a link to all reviews on Google.
  6. Emergencies we handle. Six emergency types, a call button, and a short "Not an emergency? We also handle" list for people who landed here with a smaller job.
  7. About East Atlantic. Who they are in two short paragraphs, the local office and the community groups they support. Photos of a team member at a company van and the office sign.
  8. Service area. Towns grouped by area (Wilmington and nearby, the beaches, across the river and up the coast), a call button, and a Google map of the area.
  9. Before we get there. Three short questions: what to do while you wait, whether we handle regular repairs too, and whether we handle business emergencies.
  10. Closing. "Get a plumber on the way." Call button plus a three-field callback form (name, phone, what's going on).
  11. Footer. Name, address, phone and license, matching the Google Business Profile.
  12. Mobile call bar. Full-width yellow "Call now" bar fixed to the bottom of the screen on mobile.

Design choices

  • Yellow means call. The brand yellow is used only on call buttons, so the eye learns where to tap. The callback form button is navy on purpose.
  • One phone number everywhere on the page, eight tap-to-call links in all.
  • Brand colors and logo from the live site: navy and yellow, the wrench logo. Headlines in a sturdy serif, body text in Raleway (the site's own font).
  • Real photos only. Every photo is East Atlantic's own, taken from their Google Business Profile. Their main website uses stock photos, so we skipped those.

Before this goes live

  • Photos. Get the original files from the client for the five photos used here, and confirm the people pictured are fine being on an ad page.
  • Logo file. An SVG or high-resolution logo from the client. The preview uses the logo from the current landing pages.
  • Tracking number. The page shows the main number until the Google Ads forwarding number and number swap are set up. Every call link carries a position tag (header, hero, steps, services, service area, closing, footer, call bar) so we can see which button gets the calls.
  • Form. The form here is a preview and isn't connected. On the live page it posts to GHL with hidden fields for the click ID and UTM tags.
  • License number. Confirm #27975 is current before launch.
  • Speed. Target a mobile PageSpeed score of 85+ on the live build (today's page scores 45).
